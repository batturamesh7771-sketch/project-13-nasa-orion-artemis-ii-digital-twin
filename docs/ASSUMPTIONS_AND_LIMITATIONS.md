# Engineering Assumptions & Model Limitations

## 1. Educational Digital Twin Context
This digital twin of NASA's Orion spacecraft is constructed for **aerospace engineering education, trajectory demonstration, dynamic control studies, and simulation visualization**. It is **not** flight-certified operational software and does not replace official NASA/ESA mission design systems (such as COP-7, OTIS, or POST2).

---

## 2. Telemetry Provenance & Labeling Standard
To prevent ambiguity between actual real-world measurements and mathematical simulations, all data streams and visualization elements within this project strictly adhere to the following labeling taxonomy:

* `[PUBLIC HISTORICAL DATA]`: Data derived directly from official post-flight reports or telemetry archives published by NASA/ESA (e.g. Artemis I flight summary figures).
* `[EPHEMERIS DATA]`: High-precision celestial and planetary state vectors retrieved live from the NASA/JPL Horizons API or NAIF SPICE kernels.
* `[SIMULATED]`: Deterministic output generated by the digital twin's physical equations of motion, aerodynamic models, and GNC algorithms.
* `[SYNTHETIC TELEMETRY]`: Simulated sensor signals with modeled sensor noise, quantization, packet loss, or simulated fault injections.

## 3. Modeling Assumptions by Subsystem

### 3.1 Orbital Mechanics & Trajectory
1. **Gravitational Field**: Trajectory propagation employs Cowell's formulation with Earth gravitational zonal harmonic $J_2 = 1.08263 \times 10^{-3}$, point-mass third-body lunar gravity, and point-mass solar gravity. Higher-order spherical harmonics ($J_3, J_4$ and Moon spherical harmonics) are neglected in real-time runs to maintain high iteration rates on dual-core CPU hardware.
2. **Atmospheric Model**: Earth's atmosphere is modeled using the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere piecewise exponential model up to $120\text{ km}$. Above $120\text{ km}$, vacuum is assumed.

### 3.2 Aerodynamics & Aerothermodynamics
1. **Hypersonic Aerodynamic Coefficients**: A modified Newtonian flow model is applied for the Orion Crew Module's blunt spherical-cone geometry:
   $$C_p = C_{p,max} \sin^2(\theta)$$
   Yielding hypersonic drag coefficient $C_D \approx 1.25$ and lift-to-drag ratio $L/D \approx 0.25$ with a trimmed angle of attack of $\sim -18^\circ$ to $-20^\circ$.
2. **Stagnation Point Heat Flux**: Calculated using the classic **Sutton-Graves** relation for hypersonic stagnation aerothermal heating:
   $$\dot{q}_{stag} = k_{sg} \sqrt{\frac{\rho_\infty}{R_{eff}}} V_\infty^3$$
   where $k_{sg} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}\text{ kg}^{1/2}\text{m}^{-1}$, effective nose radius $R_{eff} \approx 6.0\text{ m}$, freestream density $\rho_\infty$, and velocity $V_\infty$.
3. **Ablation Model**: Avcoat heat-shield ablation is evaluated using an effective heat of ablation ($H_{abl} \approx 12 - 16\text{ MJ/kg}$) to compute surface recession and mass loss. Coupled Navier-Stokes / non-equilibrium chemical kinetics (hypersonic CFD) are treated as future extensions.

### 3.3 Structural Mechanics
1. **Cabin Pressure Shell**: Evaluated using thin-wall membrane stress theory for an Al-Li 2195 isotropic equivalent shell ($E = 76\text{ GPa}$, $\nu = 0.33$, $\sigma_y = 350\text{ MPa}$):
   $$\sigma_{hoop} = \frac{p \cdot r}{t}, \quad \sigma_{long} = \frac{p \cdot r}{2t}$$
   Under $101.3\text{ kPa}$ ($1\text{ atm}$) internal differential pressure.
2. **Inertial Load Factors**: Verified against a nominal $3.5\text{ g}$ launch acceleration and $4.0\text{ g}$ skip-entry deceleration (with emergency limit of $11\text{ g}$). Required factor of safety is $\text{FoS} \ge 1.5$.

### 3.4 6-DOF Attitude Control & Sensor Estimation
1. **Kinematics**: Quaternion representation ($\mathbf{q} = [q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3]^T$) is used to avoid gimbal lock singularity.
2. **Actuation**: Pulse-width modulated (PWM) or deadband thruster firings modeled as ideal impulse bits for the $12 \times 160\text{ N}$ CM RCS thrusters.
3. **Navigation Sensor Suite**: Modeled as strapdown IMU (tri-axial accelerometers and gyroscopes) with Gaussian white noise and bias drift, fused with simulated Star Tracker quaternions using an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF).

## 4. Hardware Computational Bounds
Given the user's host environment (Intel Core i5-5200U dual-core CPU @ 2.2 GHz, 8 GB RAM):
* Heavy multi-million cell 3D Navier-Stokes CFD meshes and massive non-linear finite element grids are purposefully avoided.
* All physics evaluations are designed to compute in near real-time ($> 10\times$ faster than real-time for orbital propagation) within a modest RAM footprint ($< 250\text{ MB}$ for Python processes, $< 1\text{ GB}$ for MATLAB/Simulink sessions).
